Response to Trademark Objections in India Section 11(1)

By GenioBrain Experts • 5 Min Read

A trademark objection under Section 11(1) of the Trade Marks Act, 1999, is one of the most common hurdles faced by applicants in India. This objection is raised by the Trademark Examiner when the proposed mark is found to be identical or confusingly similar to an earlier trademark on the register in respect of similar goods or services.

Understanding Section 11(1)

The primary intent of Section 11 is to protect the public from confusion or deception. If your mark closely resembles an existing registered mark, the Registrar will raise an objection in the Examination Report to safeguard the rights of the prior user.

"The test of similarity is based on the perspective of an average consumer with imperfect recollection. If a mark is likely to cause confusion in their mind, it attracts an objection under Section 11."

How to File a Strong Response?

Receiving an objection doesn't mean your trademark is rejected. You have the right to file a comprehensive reply within 30 days of receiving the Examination Report. A strong response should include:

  • Differentiation of Marks: Highlight visual, phonetic, and conceptual differences between your mark and the cited marks.
  • Difference in Goods/Services: Show that your industry, target audience, or trade channels differ from the cited mark.
  • Prior Usage: If you have been using the mark before the cited mark, provide invoices and evidence of prior use.
  • Acquired Distinctiveness: Prove that your mark has gained independent reputation and goodwill in the market.

A well-drafted legal response supported by relevant case laws significantly increases the chances of overcoming a Section 11 objection and proceeding to trademark publication.
