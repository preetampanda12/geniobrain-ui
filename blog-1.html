WHAT IS STATEMENT OF WORKING FOR PATENT IN INDIA?

By GenioBrain Experts • 5 Min Read

A patent is an exclusive right granted by the government to an inventor, allowing them to prevent others from making, using, selling, or importing their invention without permission. However, with this exclusivity comes a responsibility—the responsibility to ensure the invention is actually used or "worked" for the benefit of the public. In India, this is enforced through the requirement of filing a "Statement of Working".

The Legal Requirement

Under Section 146(2) of the Indian Patents Act, 1970, and Rule 131 of the Patents Rules, 2003, every patentee and licensee is legally obligated to submit an annual statement confirming the extent to which their patented invention has been worked on a commercial scale in India.

"The primary objective of the patent system is not just to reward inventors, but to ensure that technological advancements reach the public and contribute to the economic development of the nation."

Why is it Important?

The Statement of Working is a critical compliance metric. The Indian government uses this information to ascertain whether the patent is fulfilling its socio-economic purpose. If a patent is found to be not worked in India for a period of three years from its grant, it can become vulnerable to the issuance of a Compulsory License (CL) under Section 84 of the Act. A compulsory license allows a third party to utilize the patent without the patentee's consent, ensuring that the public's needs are met at a reasonable price.

Filing Form 27

The statement is filed using Form 27. It requires detailed information regarding:

  • Whether the invention has been worked or not worked in India.
  • If not worked, the reasons for not working and the steps being taken for working the invention.
  • The approximate revenue/value accrued in India through manufacturing or importing.
  • Whether the public requirement has been met partly/adequately/to the fullest extent at a reasonable price.

Failure to file this statement, or providing false information, can lead to substantial penalties, including heavy fines and even imprisonment. Therefore, maintaining accurate records and timely submission is paramount for any patent holder in India.
